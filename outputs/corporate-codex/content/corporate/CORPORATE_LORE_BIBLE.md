# CORPORATE — Lore Bible / Working Canon

This document collects the currently approved working ideas for the CORPORATE universe.

It is not a final public lore document. It is a living design bible for the creator and for coding/design agents working on the CORPORATE codex site and game prototype.

---

## 1. Core Premise

CORPORATE is a grounded sci-fi tactical logistics game and worldbuilding project.

At the mechanical level, it is a small tactical/board-game-like system about squads, resources, pipelines, delayed replacements, sabotage, and extraction quotas.

At the worldbuilding level, it is a bitter, grounded satire about private violence, corporate language, resource wars, weak institutions, market reactions, media framing, and the disappearance of responsibility inside systems.

The core sentence:

> Corporate Codex is not a wiki. It is a market interface watching a war.

The player is not a heroic soldier on the ground. The player is closer to an office/corporate operator, manager, or executive operations controller who observes and directs violence through a terminal.

The horror is not that the world is full of cartoon villains. The horror is that normal-looking institutions and normal-looking professionals can produce atrocities through procedure, contracts, risk management, and carefully polished language.

---

## 2. Tone of the World

CORPORATE is not flashy neon cyberpunk.

It should feel grounded, administrative, financial, journalistic, and uncomfortably realistic.

The setting is scary because one can imagine its news reports, stock movements, press conferences, peacekeeping reports, public scandals, analyst notes, memes, official denials, and market reactions.

The intended realism is almost journalistic.

The world is experienced through:
- market dashboards;
- news tickers;
- public statements;
- press briefings;
- peacekeeping reports;
- financial analysis;
- legal exposure metrics;
- public image metrics;
- thermal footage leaks;
- low-frame-rate combat-zone clips;
- corporate dashboards;
- analyst notes;
- internet discourse.

Classic cyberpunk often places the player at street level: inside the city, inside the social experience, inside personal survival.

CORPORATE uses another perspective: the modern observer/operator position. People experience historical violence through news feeds, dashboards, markets, prediction bets, discourse, memes, and financial reactions. The player participates through interfaces.

This is system-level immersion rather than street-level immersion.

## 3. Setting: Proxima Centauri b

The primary conflict takes place on Proxima Centauri b.

Approved lore hooks:
- Proxima Centauri b is the closest known exoplanet candidate/setting around the nearest star system.
- It orbits an unstable red dwarf with frequent stellar flare activity.
- Stellar flares can explain:
  - communications failures;
  - sensor degradation;
  - equipment malfunctions;
  - interrupted drone feeds;
  - blackout windows;
  - tactical ambiguity;
  - unverifiable casualties;
  - delayed reporting;
  - strange anomalies on the planet.
- The short orbital period of roughly 11 Earth days is used as a lore explanation for strange accelerated local time/cycles in FRONTIER PLANET.
- Local “years” or operational cycles can be dramatically shorter than Earth-based administrative time.
- Corporations, colonists, military contractors, and administrators may use multiple time standards:
  - Earth Standard Time;
  - Proxima Local Cycle;
  - Operational Cycle;
  - Flare Watch windows.

Proxima b is not a stable state and not a fully mature legal zone. It is a frontier where capital, extraction, private force, weak administration, and public institutions arrived faster than law and political order.

## 4. Relationship to FRONTIER PLANET

CORPORATE is a prequel or sister-layer to FRONTIER PLANET.

CORPORATE takes place during the era of corporate wars over Proxima b’s valuable resource and extraction infrastructure.

FRONTIER PLANET takes place later or from another perspective, after the consequences of this conflict have reshaped the frontier.

Possible relationship:
- CORPORATE = the cold, executive, tactical, financial layer of the resource war.
- FRONTIER PLANET = the later survival/extraction sandbox on a damaged frontier world full of abandoned infrastructure, ruins, autonomous systems, corporate leftovers, and post-conflict communities.

CORPORATE shows how the trauma was administratively produced.

FRONTIER PLANET shows what remains.

## 5. Central Theme

CORPORATE is increasingly a story about the monopoly on violence.

The disturbing core question:

> What happens when the monopoly on violence disappears, but the need for order remains?

On Earth, the state at least formally claims the monopoly on legitimate force.

On Proxima b:
- the local administration claims authority but lacks force;
- peacekeepers claim mandate but lack means;
- corporations claim licenses and infrastructure rights;
- PMCs claim to provide security;
- colonists claim the right to live;
- markets reward whoever maintains extraction.

There is no single legitimate arbiter strong enough to stop the conflict.

This produces a market for violence.

Where state order fails, order becomes a service.

Where order becomes a service, war becomes a business model.

## 6. Gameplay-Worldbuilding Alignment

The mechanics are intentionally simple and board-game-compatible.

Core mechanics:
- grid/board;
- resource nodes;
- HQ/base;
- MUTO units;
- pipelines/supply lines;
- resource extraction;
- sabotage;
- repair;
- delayed replacements;
- limited roster slots;
- economic quota;
- HQ destruction;
- public/media/market consequences in the computer version.

This is not a limitation. It is the core language of the game.

Every pipeline is a claim of power.

Every resource node is a contested economic/legal/physical object.

Every replacement is depersonalization.

Every “extraction quota” is the market logic of war.

Every public visibility or legal exposure change is the world reacting to the player’s actions.

The player’s actions should be reflected as:
- tactical log;
- news headlines;
- stock movement;
- public exposure;
- legal exposure;
- insurance changes;
- public discussion snippets.

The goal is not to simulate an entire city or society. The goal is to simulate the way this world sees itself.

## 7. MUTO

MUTO = Mobile Unit for Tactical Operations.

A corporate/PMC term for mercenary/operative squads or tactical units.

Secondary meaning:
- “muto” means mute/silent in Italian, giving the acronym a cold double meaning.
- It suggests silent, deniable, depersonalized operators.

Approved tone:
- Do not say “soldier died” if the corporate interface would say “MUTO signal lost”.
- MUTO language should depersonalize people into operational modules.

Example UI language:
- MUTO-ENG-04 deployed.
- MUTO-SNP-02 signal lost.
- Replacement authorized.
- Asset group ready.
- MUTO module unavailable.

MUTO archetypes:
- Assault / area control;
- Engineer / infrastructure recovery;
- Demolition / obstacle denial;
- Scout / signal acquisition;
- Sniper / precision deterrence.

The euphemistic role names matter. They show how violence is made compatible with corporate dashboards.

## 8. Main Organizations

### EXEX — Extraterrestrial Excavation

Type: Off-world mining / resource infrastructure.

Role:
Possibly the most successful and powerful corporation in the setting. Space mining began with EXEX, and no one has surpassed them yet.

Tone:
- old monopoly;
- old money;
- old licenses;
- respectable public face;
- ruthless extraction logic;
- claims to be a mining/infrastructure company, not a military actor.

Core public position:
> EXEX is not a military organization. EXEX is an extraction company.

EXEX language:
- licensed operations;
- critical infrastructure;
- operational complexity;
- commercial confidentiality;
- stakeholder value;
- extraction continuity;
- infrastructure stabilization;
- responsible extraterrestrial development.

EXEX grows when pipelines work.

---

### OPSEC — Operational Security

Type: Private military / security contractor.

Role:
A PMC created from former soldiers and parts of a once-existing regular army. It includes veterans and new fighters equipped with the newest military technology.

Influences:
- Blackwater / private military contractors;
- Palantir-style operational data;
- defense-tech companies;
- polished consulting/business rhetoric.

Tone:
- violence as service;
- threats reframed as guarantees;
- war translated into continuity, risk, and service delivery;
- professional, calm, polished, dangerous.

Core line:
> OPSEC does not threaten. OPSEC guarantees continuity.

Another core line:
> The future is not peaceful. The future is managed.

OPSEC grows when pipelines explode, insecurity rises, and companies need protection.

---

### UNICOL — United Colonies

Type: Peacekeeping / political institution / PvE complication.

Role:
A satirical take on UN-style helplessness and institutional impotence.

UNICOL is not primarily a main playable faction. It is a map complication, PvE layer, political actor, and source of reports.

Lore:
After the conflict on Proxima b escalated, colonial governments deployed a peacekeeping contingent. Due to low funding, corruption, bureaucracy, and lack of real power, UNICOL mostly fields obsolete fragile robots with old weapons.

They cannot seriously oppose corporate operatives or modern PMCs unless they appear in numbers.

Public perception:
Many people think UNICOL on Proxima is a political stunt, a corruption vehicle, or a money-laundering scheme.

In practice, UNICOL is known mostly through news reports about another outpost, convoy, observer station, or headquarters being accidentally destroyed.

UNICOL language:
- deep concern;
- mandate-limited response;
- deconfliction corridor;
- civilian movement disruption;
- unresolved thermal signatures;
- humanitarian access remains restricted;
- all parties must exercise restraint.

UNICOL controls press releases.

---

### PSA — Proxima Settlement Authority

Type: Weak local administration / political actor.

Role:
A semi-legitimate local administration on Proxima b.

It exists as a political fact but has little real control.

It may:
- issue condemnations;
- create emergency directives;
- announce civilian corridors;
- issue or contest extraction licenses;
- demand access for investigators;
- promise solutions it cannot enforce;
- claim sovereignty over areas it cannot physically secure.

Tone:
- administrative ghost of a state;
- weak authority;
- performative sovereignty;
- official statements with little enforcement capacity.

PSA may not be a gameplay faction with units, but it can influence missions through:
- legal modifiers;
- civilian corridor rules;
- public exposure changes;
- license disputes;
- emergency directives;
- temporary restrictions;
- reputation effects.

PSA core lie:
> We control the situation.

## 9. Indexes and Market Objects

The codex site can include non-corporate market instruments.

### PXB-X — Proxima Resource Futures

Type: Commodity/resource index.

Represents the market price of the mysterious valuable resource extracted on Proxima b.

Behavior:
- rises on successful extraction;
- volatile during infrastructure attacks;
- jumps during supply restrictions;
- reacts to EXEX pipeline status.

---

### OCI — Outer Colony Insurance Index

Type: Insurance/risk index.

Represents the pricing of risk in outer colonies:
- evacuation;
- lost personnel;
- infrastructure destruction;
- flare windows;
- civilian corridors;
- legal exposure;
- unresolved deaths.

Behavior:
- rises when things get worse.

---

### ACSB — Autonomous Combat Systems Basket

Type: Defense-tech index.

Represents companies producing:
- drones;
- autonomous platforms;
- MUTO-compatible systems;
- sensor packages;
- fire-control systems;
- blackout-resilient tactical platforms.

Behavior:
- rises during conflict escalation and increased demand for autonomous systems.

## 10. Possible Placeholder Actors for Future Expansion

These are not fully approved canon yet. They are useful placeholders for future growth.

### SYNOPTIC ORBITAL

Type: Orbital imaging / sensor intelligence company.

Role:
Controls satellite/thermal feeds, “independent” verification, mapping, surveillance, and delayed/edited records.

Core question:
Who owns the eyes above Proxima?

Possible slogan:
> Nothing happens off-record.

But of course, events can happen off-record if the client pays enough.

---

### LUMEN RELAY / NEARLIGHT COMMUNICATIONS

Type: Interstellar/colonial relay and bandwidth infrastructure.

Role:
Controls communication priority during flare interference and blackout windows.

Core horror:
Even communication during disaster is monetized.

Possible UI event:
> FLARE WINDOW ACTIVE. Non-priority command refresh degraded.

---

### HALCYON RISK / COLONIAL RISK EXCHANGE

Type: Insurance and underwriting.

Role:
Determines whether a settlement, corridor, worker, or death is insurable or recognized.

Core phrase:
> Fatality recognition delayed pending signal confirmation.

---

### DOMUS HABITAT SYSTEMS

Type: Habitation, oxygen, water, and civilian infrastructure.

Role:
Builds/controls domes, housing, oxygen access, water loops, and civilian infrastructure.

Core horror:
Life as subscription.

Possible phrase:
> Oxygen access restored after payment reconciliation.

## 11. The Intended Feeling

CORPORATE should feel like:
- a finance app that accidentally became a war-crime dashboard;
- an Apple Stocks-style interface watching a resource war;
- a Reuters/Bloomberg terminal from a slightly worse future;
- a corporate dashboard where terrible things are expressed as manageable metrics;
- a board game whose simple tokens imply an enormous system behind them.

It should not feel like:
- a neon cyberpunk cockpit;
- a flashy sci-fi HUD;
- a generic lore wiki;
- a superhero/final-boss corporate dystopia;
- a world of cartoon villains.

The people in CORPORATE are human.

That is the point.

They do not need to look monstrous. They can be normal, polished, intelligent, calm, ambitious, professional, and terrifying.
