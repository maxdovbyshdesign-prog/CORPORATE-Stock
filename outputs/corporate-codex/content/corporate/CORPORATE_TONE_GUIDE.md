# CORPORATE — Tone Guide

This document defines the tone, language, interface style, and creative constraints for CORPORATE.

---

## 1. Core Tone

CORPORATE is grounded, serious, bleak, bitter, satirical, and realistic.

The world should feel like a future that could plausibly grow out of the present.

Avoid cartoon evil.

Avoid exaggerated cyberpunk villains.

Avoid neon spectacle unless there is a very specific reason.

The strongest horror in CORPORATE comes from:
- professional language;
- legal framing;
- market reactions;
- detached institutional reports;
- polished business rhetoric;
- public relations damage control;
- weak peacekeeping;
- private force;
- administrative helplessness;
- the ability of systems to make violence feel normal.

The tone is not “look how evil these people are.”

The tone is:

> Look how easily normal institutions can produce horror when language, markets, distance, and responsibility are arranged correctly.

---

## 2. Difference From Classic Cyberpunk

Classic cyberpunk often uses:
- street-level immersion;
- urban survival;
- personal stories;
- gangs and megacorps;
- neon cities;
- visible body modification;
- theatrical villains;
- social protest from below.

CORPORATE uses:
- system-level immersion;
- market feeds;
- corporate dashboards;
- news tickers;
- financial analysis;
- public image metrics;
- legal exposure;
- media discourse;
- remote operations;
- the observer/operator position.

Cyberpunk asks:
> How does a person survive inside the future city?

CORPORATE asks:
> How does a person watch, manage, profit from, and normalize someone else’s catastrophe through an interface?

---

## 3. Visual Direction

Use a grounded finance/news interface.

The site should feel closer to:
- Apple Stocks;
- Reuters;
- Bloomberg-lite;
- a clean institutional dashboard;
- a corporate internal market feed;
- a financial app from a slightly worse future.

It should not look like:
- a sci-fi cockpit;
- a neon holographic HUD;
- a hacking minigame;
- a military fantasy interface;
- a generic cyberpunk UI with glowing hexagons.

Visual traits:
- dark charcoal or near-black background;
- clean cards;
- compact typography;
- restrained color;
- small sparklines;
- financial dashboard layout;
- serious spacing;
- subtle status pills;
- restrained green/red for market movement;
- amber for warnings;
- minimal decorative sci-fi elements.

Core visual sentence:

> Make it look like a finance app that accidentally became a war-crime dashboard.

---

## 4. Interface Language

Preferred terms:
- operational continuity;
- legal exposure;
- public visibility;
- extraction quota;
- signal quality;
- flare activity;
- replacement authorized;
- asset group;
- MUTO signal lost;
- security provider;
- licensed operations;
- infrastructure incident;
- civilian movement disruption;
- unresolved thermal signatures;
- mandate-limited response;
- commercial confidentiality;
- deconfliction corridor;
- critical infrastructure;
- stakeholder confidence;
- market response.

Avoid:
- melodramatic villain language;
- obvious “evil corporation” lines;
- military macho clichés unless filtered through corporate language;
- “kill them all” style phrasing;
- lore-dump encyclopedia language;
- overly poetic high fantasy descriptions.

The best CORPORATE lines are calm and disgusting.

Example:
> Legal exposure: managed.

Example:
> OPSEC does not threaten. OPSEC guarantees continuity.

Example:
> EXEX is not a military organization. EXEX is an extraction company.

Example:
> Fatality recognition delayed pending signal confirmation.

---

## 5. Corporate Language Rules

Violence should often be reframed as service, continuity, risk, or infrastructure.

Do not say:
> We will destroy the settlement.

Say:
> The obstruction will be removed within the agreed response window.

Do not say:
> We killed civilians.

Say:
> Unresolved thermal signatures remain under review pending physical access.

Do not say:
> We are fighting a war.

Say:
> We are operating in a complex multi-actor extraction environment.

Do not say:
> We hire mercenaries.

Say:
> We contract certified security providers for critical infrastructure continuity.

Do not say:
> The peacekeepers are useless.

Say:
> UNICOL response remains limited by mandate constraints and equipment readiness.

---

## 6. Humor and Satire

The satire should be dry, bitter, and institutional.

It can be absurd, but the absurdity should feel plausible.

Good absurdity:
- a TED-style talk about monetizing the gap between law and violence;
- a peacekeeping report describing a destroyed civilian corridor as “movement disruption”;
- a stock price rising after a denial of responsibility;
- an official statement saying “we are not a military actor” while refusing to disclose security contractor logs;
- a local administration issuing a mandatory order with no enforcement capacity.

Bad absurdity:
- goofy parody names;
- cartoon villain speeches;
- giant evil logos;
- excessive jokes that break the seriousness;
- “haha capitalism bad” without specificity.

---

## 7. Media Layer

CORPORATE’s world should be communicated through media artifacts.

Useful formats:
- financial analysis;
- market ticker headlines;
- press conference transcripts;
- peacekeeping reports;
- analyst notes;
- public reaction snippets;
- leaked thermal footage captions;
- official statements;
- internal memos;
- PSA emergency directives;
- OPSEC leadership talks;
- EXEX investor calls;
- UNICOL incident reports.

The goal is not to explain the world directly.

The goal is to let the player infer the world through its documents, reports, and reactions.

---

## 8. Market as Worldbuilding

Stock prices, indexes, insurance rates, and public image are abstractions humans use to understand complex events.

In CORPORATE, this abstraction becomes the main lens.

Player action can affect:
- employer stock;
- rival stock;
- OPSEC demand;
- UNICOL credibility;
- PSA legitimacy;
- Proxima resource futures;
- outer colony insurance rates;
- autonomous combat systems index;
- public visibility;
- legal exposure.

Example chain:
- Player destroys pipeline.
- Tactical log: Enemy pipeline destroyed.
- News ticker: Explosion reported near disputed extraction corridor.
- Market: OPSEC +0.9%, EXEX -0.4%, OCI +0.3%.
- Public visibility rises if civilians or UNICOL were nearby.

---

## 9. The Player Position

The player is an operator/manager/executive controller, not a battlefield hero.

The player sees:
- tactical markers;
- thermal signatures;
- pipeline status;
- replacement queues;
- market reactions;
- headlines;
- legal exposure;
- public visibility.

The player does not need direct emotional cutscenes for every event.

The interface should make them feel the distance.

Distance is the horror.

The player should feel:
- “I clicked a tile.”
- “A pipeline exploded.”
- “A news headline appeared.”
- “The stock moved.”
- “Someone far away became an unresolved thermal signature.”

---

## 10. Design Constraints

CORPORATE must remain understandable as both:
- a board game;
- a computer game.

If a mechanic cannot be imagined as a board-game component, token, track, event card, or status, it is probably too complex for now.

Board-game-compatible components:
- grid tiles;
- MUTO units;
- HQs;
- resource nodes;
- pipelines;
- public visibility track;
- market confidence track;
- legal exposure tokens;
- media event cards;
- replacement queue;
- faction modifiers;
- civilian corridor markers;
- UNICOL patrol/robot markers;
- PSA directive cards.

The computer version can add:
- animated tickers;
- live news feed;
- charts;
- low-frame-rate thermal clips;
- automated calculations;
- AI;
- reactive market effects;
- logs and dashboards.

---

## 11. Writing Checklist

Before adding new lore text, ask:

1. Does this sound like something an institution would actually publish?
2. Is the language too obviously evil?
3. Is the horror expressed through procedure, framing, or omission?
4. Would this work as a news headline, report excerpt, investor note, or dashboard metric?
5. Does it make the world feel larger without requiring a full 3D city?
6. Does it support the themes of private violence, weak authority, resource extraction, and mediated distance?

If yes, it probably belongs in CORPORATE.
